# **What are Campaign Publishing Checks?**

  


Campaign publishing checks review your campaign for missing or incorrect information before it is sent to Google or LinkedIn. These checks help you fix common setup problems earlier so you can publish with fewer failed attempts.

  


For example, HighLevel may check for issues such as:

  * Missing headlines

  * Missing descriptions

  * Missing Website URLs

  * Missing keywords

  * Invalid campaign dates

  * Missing creative assets


  


If HighLevel finds a problem, it can stop publishing and show you what needs to be corrected first.

* * *

## **Key Benefits of Campaign Publishing Checks**

  


The improved campaign checks make it easier to understand what is wrong and fix several issues at once. This helps reduce repeated publish attempts and gives you clearer guidance before the campaign is submitted.

  


  * **See multiple errors at once:** Review several campaign issues in one list instead of discovering them one by one.  
  


  * **Get clearer error messages:** Understand what is missing or incorrect and where the problem is located.  
  


  * **Jump directly to the problem:** Click an error to go to the field or section that needs attention.  
  


  * **Fix errors faster:** Resolve several issues before trying to publish again.

  *   
**Better Google checks:** Google campaigns now go through additional checks before submission.  
  


  * **Clearer LinkedIn errors:** LinkedIn carousel and creative issues now identify the affected item more clearly.  
  


  * **Better publishing feedback:** Status messages make it easier to understand what HighLevel is checking during publishing.

## **What Happens When You Click Publish?**

  


When you click **Publish** , HighLevel checks the campaign for supported setup issues before sending it to the advertising platform. If problems are found, publishing pauses so you can fix them.

  


The basic flow is:

  1. Finish setting up the campaign.  
  


  2. Click **Publish**.  
  


  3. HighLevel checks the campaign.  
  


  4. If errors are found, the **Fix errors before publishing** prompt appears.  
  


  5. Open the error list.  
  


  6. Review the issues.  
  


  7. Fix the affected fields.  
  


  8. Publish again.

## **Jump Directly to the Field That Needs Attention**

  


Clickable error messages help you find the exact place that needs to be fixed. This is especially useful for larger campaigns with multiple ads, ad groups, creatives, or carousel cards.

  


To use an error shortcut:

  1. Open the error list.  
  


  2. Click the error you want to fix.  
  


  3. HighLevel takes you to the related field or section.  
  


  4. Correct the issue.  
  


  5. Return to the review screen and continue fixing any remaining errors.


  


This reduces the need to search through the campaign manually.

## **What Happens After You Fix an Error?**

  


After you correct campaign details, HighLevel refreshes the campaign checks so fixed issues no longer remain incorrectly listed.

  


A simple workflow is:

  1. Open the error list.  
  


  2. Fix the first issue.  
  


  3. Continue working through the remaining errors.  
  


  4. Save your campaign changes.  
  


  5. Click **Publish** again.  
  


  6. HighLevel checks the campaign again.  
  


  7. If no blocking issues remain, publishing can continue.

## **Pre-Publish Errors vs. Google or LinkedIn Rejections**

  


Passing HighLevel's campaign checks does not guarantee that Google or LinkedIn will approve the campaign. The advertising platform may still return an error or rejection after submission.

  


There are two different types of issues to understand:

  


**Pre-publish errors:** Problems HighLevel finds before the campaign is submitted, such as missing fields or incomplete ad content.

  


**Platform errors or rejections:** Problems returned by Google or LinkedIn after submission, such as policy violations or platform-specific requirements.

  


If the campaign passes HighLevel's checks but is later rejected, review the feedback returned by the advertising platform.

* * *

## **Validation Errors vs. Opportunity Score**

  


Validation errors and Opportunity Score serve different purposes. Understanding the difference helps you know which items must be fixed and which are recommendations.

  


**Validation errors:** Problems that may prevent the campaign from publishing until they are fixed.

  


**Opportunity Score:** Recommendations that can help improve campaign readiness or setup but are not the same as 

blocking publishing errors.

  


A low Opportunity Score does not necessarily mean the campaign has a publishing error.

## **How To Fix Campaign Publishing Errors**

  


Following the error list in order can help you correct campaign issues quickly and avoid repeated publish attempts. Start by opening the campaign review screen and fixing each issue shown.  
  


  1. Open the Google or LinkedIn campaign in **Ad Manager**.  
  


  2. Review the campaign, ad groups or ad sets, and ads.  
  


  3. Click **Publish**.  
  


  4. If prompted, click **Fix errors**.  
  


  5. Open the error list.  
  


  6. Review all identified issues.  
  


  7. Click an error to go directly to the affected field or section.  
  


  8. Correct the issue.  
  


  9. Repeat until all required errors are fixed.  
  


  10. Save your changes.  
  


  11. Click **Publish** again.  
  


  12. For Google campaigns, wait for the pre-publish checks to complete.  
  


  13. If the campaign passes the checks, continue with submission.

## **Troubleshooting Campaign Publishing Errors**

  


Most publishing problems can be resolved by reviewing the error list, checking required campaign fields, and trying the publish process again after saving changes.

  


**The Publish button does not complete the campaign**

Open the error list and check for required fields that still need attention.

  


**I fixed an error, but it still appears**

Save the campaign changes and run the publishing checks again. The list should refresh after the campaign is rechecked.

  


**I see several errors for one ad**

One ad can have more than one missing or incorrect field. Work through each error shown for that ad.

  


**Google or LinkedIn rejected the campaign after HighLevel showed no errors**

HighLevel's checks do not replace Google or LinkedIn's own platform and policy review. Review the rejection details returned by the advertising platform.

  


**A LinkedIn carousel error does not make sense**

Review the specific carousel card mentioned in the error and check its headline and destination URL.

  


**My campaign start date is in the past**

Update the campaign schedule to use a valid future or current start date, then publish again.

## **Frequently Asked Questions**

  


**Q: Can HighLevel show more than one campaign error at a time?**  
A: Yes. The improved error list can show multiple campaign issues together.

  


**Q: What happens when I click an error?**  
A: HighLevel takes you to the field or section related to that error so you can fix it faster.

  


**Q: Do Google campaigns receive extra checks before publishing?**  
A: Yes. Google campaigns can go through additional checks for campaign fields, advertising requirements, and platform requirements.

  


**Q: Does this work with Google Demand Gen campaigns?**  
A: Yes. Demand Gen validation includes clearer checks for supported creative requirements such as images, logos, headlines, descriptions, videos, and carousel assets.

  


**Q: Are LinkedIn carousel cards checked individually?**  
A: The improved LinkedIn error experience provides clearer context about the specific carousel card that needs attention.

  


**Q: Will fixed errors disappear from the list?**  
A: After campaign details are corrected and rechecked, resolved errors should refresh correctly.

  


**Q: Can Google or LinkedIn still reject a campaign after it passes HighLevel's checks?**  
A: Yes. The advertising platform can still return policy or platform-level errors after submission.

  


**Q: Is Opportunity Score the same as a publishing error?**  
A: No. Opportunity Score provides recommendations, while publishing errors identify issues that may need to be fixed before the campaign can be submitted.
